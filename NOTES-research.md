# Research notes for future oracles

Checked notes from a chat the owner shared (2026-10-23). "Sound" items rest on an ancient source; "unsupported" items are
popular claims with no ancient evidence and must not go into the app as fact.

## Janus and the coin (also used in Roman Games' coin game)

- **Sound.** Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 1.7.19-22: Saturn arrived in Italy by ship and was welcomed by King Janus; Janus
  was the first to strike bronze coins, with his two-faced head on one side and a ship on the other in Saturn's honour.
  Boys still tossed coins calling *capita aut navia*, "heads or ships", long after the designs had changed.
- **Sound.** The Republican bronze *as*: laureate Janus with the value mark I; a prow with I and ROMA.
- **Sound (later history).** Medieval English "cross and pile", modern "heads or tails"; the 1845 coin toss that named
  Portland, Oregon (the "Portland Penny") is a modern footnote.
- **Unsupported.** That the toss was legally binding for crimes, property or marriage; that it decided naval battles;
  that heads was "divine favour"; that players didn't call a side; that Caesar "replaced" Janus with his own head as a
  claim to divine fate (Caesar was the first living Roman on coins, in 44 BC, on silver denarii, not on the Janus *as*).
- **Idea for the app.** A quick yes/no "coin of Janus" oracle, framed honestly: Romans tossed coins to decide things,
  but no source gives a ritual or prayer for it, so the page should say the framing is ours.

## The lots of Fortuna at Praeneste (the planned "Lots of Fortuna" oracle)

- **Sound.** Cicero, *On Divination* 2.85-87: after a dream, Numerius Suffustius split a rock at Praeneste and found
  oak lots carved with ancient letters. An olive tree on the site dripped honey; a chest for the lots was made from its
  wood. The lots are mixed and drawn by a boy, "at Fortuna's prompting". Cicero also describes Fortuna's statue with the
  infants Jupiter and Juno in her lap. (Cicero is sceptical: he asks why anyone still believes in them.)
- **Sound.** Suetonius, *Tiberius* 63: Tiberius had the Praenestine lots sealed in a chest and brought to Rome; they
  could not be found in it until the chest was returned to the temple.
- **Check before use.** That the Praenestine calendar marks Fortuna Primigenia's festival on 10-11 April (likely, but
  verify against the Fasti Praenestini text).
- **Modern interpretation, label it so.** That the drawing waited for a "nod" of the statue, and that a refusal meant
  "no answer today"; that the boy stood for the infant Jupiter. Both make good app mechanics if marked as reconstruction.
- **Real surviving lots.** A set of bronze lots inscribed in old Latin from Italy (CIL I² 2173-2189) is the closest
  evidence for what lot texts looked like. Find an edition before building.

## Other lot customs

- **Sound.** Lottery gifts at dinners: Suetonius, *Augustus* 75 (Augustus's Saturnalia gifts by lot) and Petronius,
  *Satyricon* 56 (Trimalchio's punning lottery tickets). Good for Roman Games' Saturnalia.
- **Sound.** The *sitella*, an urn of water used to draw lots in Roman assemblies (to set voting order), not an oracle.
- **Unsupported or wrong in that chat.** "Sortes Astragalorum ... 625 combinations ... Fortuna": the five-knucklebone
  oracle has 56 answers, and the Kibyra stone names Hermes as its speaker, for Apollo. A named god "Sors", "sortes
  viae" and a Statius reference to Tibur also need a source before use.
- **Pattern worth keeping (as a guide, not a rule).** Lots of chance lean to Fortuna and Hermes; lots of inspired text
  (Homer, Virgil, the Sibylline books) lean to Apollo. Our Homer oracle's prayer is to Apollo, which fits.
