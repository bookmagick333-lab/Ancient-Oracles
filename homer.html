The Homer Oracle

Think of your question. Then throw the die three times: the three numbers point to one line of Homer, and that line is the gods' answer. This is how it was done with a magical papyrus from Roman Egypt, about 1,700 years ago.

The papyrus waits for your throw.

How to consult

  1. Think of one clear question, and keep it in mind.
  2. Throw the die three times. Each throw gives a number from 1 to 6.
  3. The three numbers, in order, name one of 216 lines of Homer (6 × 6 × 6).
  4. Read the line as the answer to your question. Its meaning is for you to work out, as it was for the ancients.

Your answers

    How do we know?
    • ✔ A real oracle. The list survives on a papyrus roll from Roman Egypt, written in the 3rd century AD and now in the British Museum (Papyrus 121). It begins with 216 single lines from the Iliad and Odyssey, each numbered by three throws of a die. Two other copies survive, from Oxyrhynchus and Bologna.
    • ✔ Three throws of one die. The instructions survive in the Oxyrhynchus copy. The papyrus calls itself Homeromanteion, "Homer oracle".
    • ✔ Homer as scripture. Greeks treated Homer's poems as a source of wisdom, and lines picked at random could be read as messages from the gods.
    • ✔ Lost lines. The beginning of the roll is damaged, so 25 of the 216 lines are missing. If you throw one, the oracle is silent.
    • ? The ritual around it (prayers, purification, which god was asked) is only partly known. Here you simply think of your question.
