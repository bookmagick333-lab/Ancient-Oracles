The Marketplace Oracle

In the market square of Kibyra stood a stone pillar with a statue of Hermes. You threw five knucklebones, added up the sides, and found your answer carved on the stone: 56 answers, each spoken by a god. Ask about a journey, a sale, an illness or a plan.

The stone waits for your throw.

How to consult

  1. Think of one clear question: the stone speaks most often of journeys, buying and selling, illness, lawsuits and plans.
  2. Throw the five knucklebones together.
  3. Each bone shows one of four sides, worth 1, 3, 4 or 6. Add them up.
  4. Find your throw on the stone. It tells you which god answers, then gives the god's words.

Your answers

    How do we know?
    • ✔ A real stone. The pillar was found in 2024 in the agora of Kibyra, in south-west Türkiye, built into the wall of a later chapel. It is carved on all four sides and dates from the 2nd or 3rd century AD.
    • ✔ One of many. More than a dozen towns in the region set up the same oracle in their markets, with almost the same words. A rich citizen paid for the Kibyra one, with a statue of Hermes beside it.
    • ✔ How it works. Each answer begins with the numbers thrown, their total and the god's name; then a line describing the throw; then the god's answer in verse. Five sixes, the best throw, belongs to Hermes himself.
    • ✔ Apollo and Hermes. The oracle says its words come from Apollo of Delphi, but dice and lots were Hermes' gift, so Hermes "speaks" them from his pillar.
    • ✔ Broken places. The tops and bottoms of the stone's faces are lost, so 11 answers are missing or only scraps survive. If you throw one, the stone is silent.
    • ? How the bones fall. Real knucklebones land on their broad sides (3 and 4) far more often than their narrow ones (1 and 6). We use roughly that balance; the exact odds varied from bone to bone.
